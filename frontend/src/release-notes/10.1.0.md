## Highlights

Until now the health check only remembered a service's **current** status — is it up *right now*,
how fast did it answer the *last* probe. It couldn't answer the questions monitoring is actually
for: *what's my uptime this week?* and *when did this thing last flap?*

**10.1** fixes that. Stashboard now keeps a bounded **uptime history** for every service, surfaced
on a dedicated **Uptime** tab in the service modal:

- **Uptime %** over the last **24 hours, 7 days and 30 days**
- A **response-time sparkline** so you can see the latency trend at a glance
- An **incident log** — every past Down→Up span with its duration, and the current outage flagged
  *ongoing* if the service is down right now

History is tracked **per URL** — your main URL and the optional additional URL each get their own
uptime, sparkline and incident list.

## The details

### It records smartly, not constantly

A naïve history would write one row per service per probe forever and bloat your database. Instead,
Stashboard records a row **only when the status changes** (up ↔ down) or, between changes, at a
**sampled cadence** (default every 15 minutes) just to keep a latency trend. So a service that's
been quietly up for a month leaves a tidy trail, not a row every 60 seconds.

Both the background scan and the manual **Check now** button feed the same history.

### It never grows unbounded

A background prune drops anything older than your **retention window** — **90 days** by default. The
table stays bounded automatically; you never have to clean it up.

### Two new settings

**Settings → Health checks** gains two knobs next to the existing offline-alert tuning:

- **Uptime history retention (days)** — how long to keep the history (default 90).
- **Response-time sample interval (minutes)** — how often to sample latency between status changes
  (default 15). Status *changes* are always recorded regardless.

Like the other health-check settings, these are stored in the database and can be seeded on first
run with `STASHBOARD_HealthCheck__HistoryRetentionDays` and
`STASHBOARD_HealthCheck__HistorySampleIntervalMinutes`.

### Backups

The uptime **history** itself is runtime telemetry — it's rebuilt continuously on the live instance,
so it is deliberately **not** included in backup/restore, the same as every other live status. Your
retention and sample settings are app-wide health-check settings.

## How to use it

There's nothing to turn on — history starts accumulating on the next scan.

1. Open any service and go to the new **Uptime** tab. New services will say there's no history yet;
   hit **Check now** on the Healthcheck tab or wait for the next scan and it begins filling in.
2. As probes accumulate you'll see the **24 h / 7 d / 30 d** uptime fill in, the **response-time
   sparkline** take shape, and any downtime show up in the **incident log**.
3. To change how long history is kept or how often latency is sampled, visit **Settings → Health
   checks**.

## Out of scope

- **SLA reports / CSV or PDF exports** — the tab shows live history, not formal reports.
- **Alerting on uptime %** — offline alerts still come from the existing failure-threshold logic
  (Settings → Health checks); this phase doesn't add a separate "uptime dropped below X%" alert.
- **Long-term downsampling** — a single bounded window is enough; there are no rollup tables.
