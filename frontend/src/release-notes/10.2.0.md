## Highlights

Until now Stashboard was a private admin view — everything behind your login. **10.2** turns it into
something you can hand to family, flatmates or teammates: a **public status page** showing the live
status and uptime of a hand-picked set of your services, in the spirit of Uptime Kuma.

You assemble one or more pages, each a named selection of **your own** services, give it a public
link, and flip it **Published** — and anyone with the link sees:

- Each service's **live status** (operational / degraded / down)
- A **30-day uptime history bar** at a glance
- **Uptime %** over the last **24 hours, 7 days and 30 days**
- An aggregate **"All systems operational"** banner across the whole page

No account, no login — just the link.

## The details

### You choose exactly what's shown — and what isn't

A status page never exposes your internals. The public view shows **only** the display name, status,
uptime % and the history bar. It **never** leaks your service URLs, credentials, notes, categories,
tags, or any Docker / Proxmox details — those simply aren't in the data the public page receives.

Each service on a page can have an optional **display name** override, so the public view can say
"Media Server" even if you named it `prod-jellyfin-01` internally. Leave it blank and it falls back
to the service name.

### Private until you publish

A new page is a **draft**. While it's a draft, its link returns a plain "not found" — exactly the
same response an unknown link gives, so nobody can probe around and discover your unpublished pages.
Only when you switch **Published** on does the link go live. Switch it back off any time to take it
down.

### Built to be shared safely

The public endpoint is **rate-limited** and lightly **cached**, so even a link that gets passed
around widely can't be used to hammer your instance.

### It travels with your backups

Your status pages — their service selection, display names and publish state — are included in
**Backup / Restore**, so they survive a migration to a new instance.

## Turning it on

Status pages live under a new **Settings → Status pages** sub-page. To publish one:

1. Go to **Settings → Status pages** and click **New status page**.
2. Give it a **title**, an optional description, and pick the services to show (with optional public
   display names).
3. Set the **public link** (slug) — it auto-fills from the title; you can edit it.
4. Tick **Published** — the page is a draft and **off by default** until you do.
5. Click **Create page**, then use the **copy-link** button on the page's row to grab the public URL
   (`/status/<your-slug>`) and share it. The **open** button next to it previews the live page.

You can edit, re-publish, unpublish or delete a page any time from the same screen.

## Not included yet

This release renders **live status + history only**. Custom domains / white-labelling, email or RSS
subscriber digests, and owner-authored incident write-ups or scheduled-maintenance banners are
possible later phases — not part of 10.2.
