## Highlights

Until now every Stashboard alert — a service going offline, a Docker update appearing, Proxmox
updates piling up, a node-health alert — went out over exactly two channels: **email** and
**Telegram**. The most-requested addition is the rest of them: **Discord, ntfy, Gotify, Slack,
and generic webhooks**.

**10.0** adds an **Apprise** channel that covers all of those (and 90+ more) at once. Instead of
Stashboard hand-rolling each provider's API, it makes **one HTTP POST** to your own
[Apprise](https://github.com/caronc/apprise-api) instance (or the public stateless endpoint),
which fans the message out to every service you've configured.

It sits **alongside** email and Telegram, not instead of them — every channel is independent, so
turning Apprise on changes nothing about your existing alerts.

## The details

### One channel, every service

Apprise speaks dozens of provider "URLs" — `discord://…`, `ntfy://…`, `gotify://…`,
`slack://…`, and many more. You list the ones you want, and each notification is delivered to all
of them in a single request. Add a target by pasting its Apprise URL; remove it by deleting the
line. No per-provider setup inside Stashboard.

### Independent, and it won't drop your other alerts

Apprise is a **fully independent channel**. Each notification carries its own per-channel
throttle key that's stamped **only after a successful send** — so if your Apprise instance is
briefly down, the alert simply retries on the next check, and it **never** suppresses the email
or Telegram copy (and vice-versa). You still get exactly one notification per channel per state
change, the same as before.

### Per-target toggles, just like Telegram

Every place that already has a **Telegram** toggle now has an **Apprise** toggle next to it — on
each Docker watch and each Proxmox host. Service offline-alerts fan out through Apprise whenever
that service's "send offline notifications" switch is on. The Apprise toggle stays **disabled
until you've configured the channel**, exactly like the Telegram toggle does today.

### A "Send test" button

Once configured, a **Send test** button on the Notifications page fires a sample notification
through **each** target individually and reports **per-target** success or failure — so you can
confirm your Discord webhook works before you wait for a real outage.

### Your secrets stay secret

Apprise URLs often embed tokens, so the whole list is **encrypted at rest** (AES-256-GCM) and is
**never returned** by the API — the page shows only how many URLs are stored and their
non-secret schemes (e.g. `discord`, `ntfy`). The config and your per-target toggles are included
in **backup/restore**, re-encrypted on import so a backup stays portable across instances.

## How to turn it on

Apprise is **off by default**. To enable it:

1. You need a reachable **Apprise API** — either your own self-hosted
   [`caronc/apprise-api`](https://github.com/caronc/apprise-api) container, or the public
   stateless endpoint. Stashboard does **not** run Apprise for you.
2. Go to **Account → Notifications** and open the **Apprise notifications** card.
3. Tick **Enable Apprise notifications**.
4. Set the **Apprise base URL** — your Apprise API root (e.g. `http://apprise:8000`) or the full
   `/notify` endpoint.
5. In **Apprise URLs**, paste **one Apprise URL per line** (e.g.
   `discord://webhook_id/webhook_token`, `ntfy://ntfy.sh/my-topic`,
   `gotify://gotify.example.com/token`).
6. Click **Save Apprise settings**, then **Send test** to confirm each target works.
7. Finally, turn on the **Apprise** toggle where you want it: on a **Docker watch** (the watch
   modal), on a **Proxmox host** (the connection modal), and/or leave a service's offline-
   notifications switch on. These toggles are disabled until steps 2–6 are done.

## Out of scope

- **Running Apprise for you** — point Stashboard at your own instance or the public endpoint.
- **Per-notification routing rules** (e.g. "criticals to Discord, info to ntfy") — for now every
  notification fans out to all configured targets, the same way email + Telegram already do.
