## Highlights

Stashboard holds the keys to your homelab: it stores credentials (encrypted at rest), can open a
**shell on your Docker host**, **exec into containers**, and **recreate or destroy** containers and
LXCs. Until now, all of that sat behind a single password. **10.3** closes that gap with
**two-factor authentication (TOTP)** — the same six-digit codes you already use with Google
Authenticator, Authy, 1Password, Bitwarden and friends.

Turn it on and every sign-in asks for a code from your phone **in addition to** your password. Lose
the phone? A set of one-time **recovery codes** gets you back in.

## The details

### Enroll in seconds

Under **Account → Two-factor authentication**, click **Enable**. Stashboard shows a **QR code** (and
a manual key, if you'd rather type it) to add to your authenticator app, then asks for the first
six-digit code to confirm the app and your server agree. Your secret is **encrypted at rest** and
**never leaves the server** after enrollment — the only thing the browser ever sees is the QR for
that one setup step.

### A second step at sign-in

Once enabled, signing in becomes two steps: your password, then your authenticator code. Wrong codes
are **rate-limited** and count toward the **same account lockout** as wrong passwords, so the
six-digit space can't be brute-forced. A used code can't be replayed.

### Recovery codes for a lost device

Enabling 2FA gives you **ten one-time recovery codes**, shown **once** — save them somewhere safe.
Each works exactly once in place of an authenticator code. You can **regenerate** them any time from
the Account page (which invalidates the old set).

### Built on the existing session model

Disabling 2FA and regenerating recovery codes are security-sensitive actions: both require your
**current password** and **sign out every session**, exactly like changing your password or email.

### It travels with your backups

Your 2FA state — the enabled flag, the (encrypted) secret and your recovery codes — is included in
**Backup / Restore**, so a restored account keeps 2FA working with the very same authenticator app.

## Turning it on

2FA is **off by default** and entirely **per-account** — you control your own.

1. Go to **Account → Two-factor authentication** and click **Enable two-factor authentication**.
2. Scan the **QR code** with your authenticator app (or enter the **manual key**).
3. Type the **6-digit code** from the app and click **Verify & enable**.
4. **Save the ten recovery codes** shown on the next screen — they won't be shown again.

From then on, every sign-in asks for a code. To turn it off, use **Disable two-factor
authentication** on the same page (it asks for your password and signs you out everywhere).

## Not included yet

This release covers **authenticator-app (TOTP)** 2FA. WebAuthn / passkeys / hardware security keys,
and SMS or email one-time codes, are possible later phases — not part of 10.3.
